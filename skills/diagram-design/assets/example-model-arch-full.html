Sparse-attention encoder–decoder · 40 layers

Forty layers drawn as seventeen blocks. The multipliers carry the depth, so the figure stays at the size a reader can hold — and the one accent marks the hand-off that lets prefill stop halfway up.

Why the multipliers

Depth without forty boxes

Two sliding-window layers, then three repeats of one full sparse-attention layer plus five reuse layers: 2 + 3×6 = 20. The decoder is one full layer plus three reuse, then four repeats of reindex plus three reuse: 4 + 4×4 = 20. Drawing each layer would say nothing the multiplier does not.

What the fills mean

Three degrees of work

A full layer computes and stores its key-value cache. A reindex layer inherits that cache and scores its own selection. A reuse layer inherits both and runs attention on an inherited selection: it adds no new state, which is why it is the palest of the three.

The one accent

Encoder output

Every decoder layer projects its global cache from this single hidden state instead of from its own input. That is what lets the prompt stop at layer 20, and it is the only claim in the figure worth colouring.
